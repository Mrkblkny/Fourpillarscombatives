The Four Pillars Combatives Self-Defense Curriculum

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN.

Our self-defense curriculum is organized into five sections designed to develop practical self-defense knowledge, fighting skills, physical fitness, and emergency preparedness.

How the curriculum works: These five sections describe what students can expect to learn—not five separate weekly classes or memberships. Training opportunities include group classes, private lessons, workshops and online lessons as they are released.

01

ENVIRONMENTAL & SITUATIONAL AWARENESS

Learn to recognize potential threats, understand how criminals identify and select victims, manage unwanted contacts, avoid dangerous situations, and respond appropriately to developing threats.

02

EMPTY-HAND FIGHTING IN A WEAPONS ENVIRONMENT

Learn offensive and defensive fighting skills at different combat ranges, including striking, wrestling, clinch fighting, and ground fighting, while accounting for the possible presence of weapons.

03

WEAPONS FIGHTING & WEAPON DEFENSE

Learn which defensive weapons to consider carrying every day and how to use them safely and lawfully. Explore fighting with and defending against weapons, defensive shooting at longer distances and in close-contact situations including 0–5 feet and the clinch, weapon access, preventing an attacker from accessing weapons, weapon retention, and disarming.

04

FITNESS & ATTRIBUTES TRAINING

Develop the strength, power, endurance, mobility, speed, balance, coordination, and other physical attributes that support effective self-defense.

05

EMERGENCY MEDICAL & TRAUMA CARE

Learn essential first aid, bleeding control, emergency trauma care, CPR, medical equipment fundamentals, basic Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC) concepts, and how to respond to injuries and emergencies. This section provides educational instruction only and does not award certifications.

Medical and trauma care lessons will be added during public beta.

Close-quarters awareness and fighting capability

YOU CARRY A FIREARM. CAN YOU FIGHT WITHOUT IT—AND WITH IT AT CLOSE RANGE?

Carrying a firearm does not replace empty-hand fighting skills. You should be prepared to protect yourself without relying on it, while also understanding the added risks of physical contact at close range when you are armed. Striking, wrestling, clinch fighting, balance, awareness, lawful decisions, and safe disengagement all matter.

Why 0–5 feet matters: A violent confrontation can unfold at contact distance. Someone may already be within reach when the problem begins. That is why our training considers standing, clinch, ground, weapon-related risks, and safe disengagement together. We do not claim that most attacks occur within any single distance.

01

EMPTY-HAND SKILLS STILL MATTER

Build movement, balance, practical striking, wrestling, and grappling ability for the moments when distance disappears and you cannot simply walk away.

02

WEAPON RISKS CHANGE THE PROBLEM

WBE brings together skills developed in Empty-Hand Fighting in a Weapons Environment and Weapons. Students practice recognizing weapon risks, maintaining control, and making safer decisions during unwanted physical contact.

Different goals. Valuable skills.

COMBAT SPORTS VS. SELF-DEFENSE.

Combat sports build the skills to compete against resistance. Self-defense uses fighting ability in service of a different purpose: avoiding violence when possible and protecting lives when it cannot be avoided. Both matter, but the objectives are not the same.

If your goal is to compete in MMA, boxing, Muay Thai, wrestling, or submission grappling, we can focus coaching on your sport, its rules, skill development against resistance, conditioning, and preparation for competition. You do not have to be interested in self-defense to request combat-sports coaching. Eskrima is also available through private lessons and hosted seminars.

SPORT

COMBAT SPORTS

The goal is to beat your opponent within the rules.

Boxing, Muay Thai, wrestling, MMA, and submission grappling build timing, distance, balance, toughness, conditioning, and practical skills against a resisting opponent. They are valuable foundations.

A match has an agreed setting, rules, officials, and a known opponent. If your goal is competition, we can tailor private lessons and seminars toward improving sport-specific performance and preparing to compete.

SAFETY

SELF-DEFENSE

The goal is to avoid fighting unless you have to—and get away as soon as safely possible.

There may be no warning, no rules, and no guarantee that an aggressor is unarmed or alone. If you must fight, your skills must adapt to the situation—standing, clinching, on the ground, or around weapon-related risks. Protect yourself and others when necessary, use only necessary and lawful force, stop the immediate threat, and leave as soon as it is safe.

Combat-sports skills are an essential foundation, but awareness, contact management, decision-making, weapons awareness, and a safe exit plan complete the picture.

At Four Pillars Combatives: Weekly group classes focus on self-defense for private citizens and others seeking those skills. Dedicated boxing, Muay Thai, MMA, wrestling and grappling coaching—including competition preparation—is available by private lesson or custom seminar, not as separate regularly scheduled group classes. Professional groups can inquire about job-specific instruction.

Training is a practice, not a checkbox

SKILL IS PERISHABLE. KEEP IT SHARP.

A class introduces skills; ongoing practice helps keep them useful. Timing, distance, striking, grappling, judgment and conditioning can fade without training—whether your goal is self-defense, professional readiness or sport.

01 / AWARENESS

Keep noticing.

Make observation, boundary-setting, and choosing an exit part of your normal habits—not something you only think about during a class.

02 / PHYSICAL SKILLS

Keep practicing.

Footwork, positioning, striking, grappling, standing up, and controlled decision-making benefit from regular refreshers and realistic but safe practice.

03 / FITNESS

Keep your body ready.

Strength, endurance, balance, and mobility improve with consistent training and can decline when neglected. Fitness supports your defensive options.

Build a sustainable habit: A practical starting target is 1–3 hours per week on martial skills and 1–3 hours on fitness, adjusted to your experience, health, and schedule. Consistency matters more than an occasional intense session.
