The Four Pillars Combatives self-defense curriculum

WHAT YOU'LL TRAIN TO DO.

What if a person becomes aggressive in a parking lot, a workplace interaction escalates, or someone grabs you before you can leave? The goal is to recognize danger early, respond responsibly when necessary, and find a way to safety. Here's what each curriculum section helps you practice.

How the curriculum works: These four sections describe the skills we develop, not four separate weekly classes or memberships. Group classes teach core self-defense foundations. Specialized combat sports, weapons-based topics, and individual fitness coaching are available through private lessons, personal training, or seminars by request.

01

SITUATIONAL AWARENESS

The situation: Someone follows you through a parking lot, ignores a clear boundary, or keeps approaching after you tell them to stop. What clues would you notice—and how would you keep a way out?

Recognize problems early and make choices that help you avoid a fight.

  • Notice warning signs and changing situations before trouble reaches you.
  • Set clear boundaries, manage unwanted contact, and keep a path to safety in mind.
  • Practice making safer decisions when someone closes distance, ignores boundaries, or catches you off guard.

Awareness, communication, and escape skills also form a foundation of our women's self-defense instruction.

02

EMPTY-HAND FIGHTING

The situation: Someone grabs your clothing, pushes you against a wall, or you end up on the ground. What if leaving isn't immediately possible?

Build the striking and grappling skills to protect yourself and create a chance to leave.

  • Develop practical tools for standing confrontations using boxing, Muay Thai, wrestling, and related training.
  • Learn to protect your position, move under pressure, and break away from unwanted contact.
  • Practice recovering from the ground and getting back to your feet when possible.

We train with awareness that weapons or additional attackers may change the situation. Dedicated MMA and combat-sports coaching is available by private lesson or seminar.

03

WEAPONS FIGHTING

The situation: A person may have a weapon, or you carry one for work or personal protection and a confrontation becomes physical. How do you recognize the added risk and make safer decisions?

Understand how a weapon changes the threat—and how to make responsible decisions.

  • Recognize the possibility of an armed threat and prioritize avoidance and safe escape.
  • Develop awareness of weapon control, retention, and personal safety when contact cannot be avoided.
  • Learn principles of responsible, lawful defensive-weapon use in appropriate training settings.

Our weapons-based training draws on Eskrima and modern combatives. Specialized Weapons-Based Entanglement (WBE) connects this section with Empty-Hand Fighting and is available by request; it is not an additional curriculum section or a standing weekly class.

04

FITNESS & ATTRIBUTES TRAINING

The situation: You have to move quickly, regain your balance, or keep functioning during a physically demanding incident. Can your body support what you're asking it to do?

Improve the physical qualities that support your movement, resilience, and training.

  • Build strength and power for more confident, capable movement.
  • Develop conditioning to keep working when effort becomes demanding.
  • Improve mobility, balance, coordination, speed, and other useful physical attributes.

Fitness supports the rest of the curriculum. One-on-one personal training is also available as a separate service, whether or not you train martial arts.

Different goals. Valuable skills.

COMBAT SPORTS VS. SELF-DEFENSE.

Both can develop useful fighting skills, but they prepare you for different goals. Understanding the difference changes how you train and how you make decisions.

Want to train specifically in boxing, MMA, Muay Thai, wrestling, submission grappling or Eskrima? We also offer these subjects through private instruction and hosted seminars, with training tailored to self-defense or sport goals as appropriate.

SPORT

COMBAT SPORTS

The goal is to beat your opponent within the rules.

Boxing, Muay Thai, wrestling, MMA, and submission grappling build timing, distance, balance, toughness, conditioning, and practical skills against a resisting opponent. They are valuable foundations.

A match has an agreed setting, rules, officials, and a known opponent. Training can focus on performance and competition.

SAFETY

SELF-DEFENSE

The goal is to avoid fighting unless you have to—and get away as soon as safely possible.

There may be no warning, no rules, and no guarantee that someone is unarmed or acting alone. Awareness, boundaries, judgment, lawful responses, escape, and protecting others can matter as much as physical technique.

Combat-sports skills support self-defense, but they are not the entire plan.

At Four Pillars Combatives: Weekly group classes focus on self-defense. Dedicated boxing, Muay Thai, MMA and grappling coaching are available by private lesson or custom seminar request—not as separate regularly scheduled group classes.

Training is a practice, not a checkbox

SKILL IS PERISHABLE. KEEP IT SHARP.

A single class can show you what to do. Repeated, thoughtful practice helps you remember it, use it more reliably, and adapt when circumstances change. This matters for personal safety and for people who may face confrontation at work.

01 / AWARENESS

Keep noticing.

Make observation, boundary-setting, and choosing an exit part of your normal habits—not something you only think about during a class.

02 / PHYSICAL SKILLS

Keep practicing.

Footwork, positioning, striking, grappling, standing up, and controlled decision-making benefit from regular refreshers and realistic but safe practice.

03 / FITNESS

Keep your body ready.

Strength, endurance, balance, and mobility improve with consistent training and can decline when neglected. Fitness supports your defensive options.

Build a sustainable habit: A practical starting target is 1–3 hours per week on martial skills and 1–3 hours on fitness, adjusted to your experience, health, and schedule. Consistency matters more than an occasional intense session.
