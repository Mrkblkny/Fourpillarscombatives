The mission

AVOID THE FIGHT.
BE READY IF YOU CAN'T.

Awareness. Contact Management. Respond. Get Home Safe.

The goal is to avoid fighting unless you have to—and get away as soon as safely possible. Recognize danger, manage contact, set boundaries, and leave when you can. If you must fight, be capable of defending yourself wherever the confrontation goes: on your feet, in the clinch, or on the ground. Train to protect yourself or others when necessary, stop the immediate threat, and disengage safely.

How we train ↗

For your life. For your work.

WHAT WOULD YOU DO IF IT HAPPENED TO YOU?

You do not have to want a fight to need the skills to handle one. Whether you want to protect yourself, help someone you care about, or be better prepared for confrontation at work, we connect awareness and sound decisions with practical fighting ability when avoiding violence is no longer an option.

EVERYDAY SAFETY

Someone won't leave you alone.

At a gas station or in a parking lot, a stranger ignores your boundaries and keeps closing distance. Learn to recognize the warning signs, communicate clearly, keep space, and look for a safe exit.

ON THE JOB

A routine interaction escalates.

A customer, patient, inmate, or member of the public grows aggressive while you are doing your job. Learn to recognize escalation, manage distance and boundaries, and prepare for physical contact if necessary—while acting within your responsibilities, policies, and the law.

WHEN IT TURNS PHYSICAL

Someone grabs or knocks you down.

What if you can't simply walk away? Practice striking, grappling, and ground-survival fundamentals with an emphasis on protecting yourself, getting back up, and disengaging when possible.

A firearm is not a substitute for fighting skills

CARRYING A FIREARM DOESN'T REPLACE FIGHTING SKILLS.

A confrontation can turn physical before you have room to react. Carrying a firearm does not replace striking, wrestling, grappling, or awareness. You need empty-hand skills when you cannot rely on a firearm—and sound judgment and control when physical contact happens while you are armed at close range.

01 / CONTACT DISTANCE

WHAT IF IT STARTS AT 0–5 FEET?

At arm's length, someone may grab you, crowd you, or force a clinch. Train for contact, movement, balance, and making safer decisions—not just ideal conditions at a distance.

02 / WEAPON AWARENESS

BEING ARMED IS NOT THE SAME AS BEING PREPARED.

When physical fighting and weapons risks overlap, you need more than marksmanship. We connect empty-hand skills with awareness, weapon retention concepts, responsible decisions, and safe disengagement.

03 / CITIZENS & PROFESSIONALS

TRAIN FOR YOUR LIFE. TRAIN FOR YOUR JOB.

Private citizens can train to protect themselves and others. Law enforcement, corrections, security, and military personnel can request instruction tailored to their responsibilities, equipment, policies, and the law.

The self-defense curriculum

FIVE SECTIONS.
ONE PURPOSE.

Our self-defense curriculum is organized into five sections designed to develop practical self-defense knowledge, fighting skills, physical fitness, and emergency preparedness.

01

ENVIRONMENTAL & SITUATIONAL AWARENESS

Learn to recognize potential threats, understand how criminals identify and select victims, manage unwanted contacts, avoid dangerous situations, and respond appropriately to developing threats.

02

EMPTY-HAND FIGHTING IN A WEAPONS ENVIRONMENT

Learn offensive and defensive fighting skills at different combat ranges, including striking, wrestling, clinch fighting, and ground fighting, while accounting for the possible presence of weapons.

03

WEAPONS FIGHTING & WEAPON DEFENSE

Learn which defensive weapons to consider carrying every day and how to use them safely and lawfully. Explore fighting with and defending against weapons, defensive shooting at longer distances and in close-contact situations including 0–5 feet and the clinch, weapon access, preventing attacker weapon access, weapon retention, and disarming.

04

FITNESS & ATTRIBUTES TRAINING

Develop the strength, power, endurance, mobility, speed, balance, coordination, and other physical attributes that support effective self-defense.

05

EMERGENCY MEDICAL & TRAUMA CARE

Learn essential first aid, bleeding control, emergency trauma care, CPR, medical equipment fundamentals, and how to respond to injuries and emergencies. Medical lessons coming soon during public beta.

Our Philosophy: Recognize danger. Avoid unnecessary confrontation. Defend yourself when necessary. Provide emergency care when needed. Get Home Safe.

More than a one-time lesson

KNOW THE DIFFERENCE. KEEP YOUR SKILLS.

Learning a technique is a start. Understanding when to use it—and practicing it enough to respond under pressure—is what makes training valuable.

01

COMBAT SPORTS VS. SELF-DEFENSE

Combat sports train you to compete against an opponent within a ruleset. Self-defense prioritizes avoiding a fight when possible. If violence is unavoidable, the goal is to defend yourself or others as necessary, end the immediate threat, and get away as soon as safely possible.

Want to compete? We also offer combat-sports private lessons and seminars, including MMA, boxing, Muay Thai, wrestling, and grappling. You can train specifically for competition—not just for self-defense.

When the goal is self-defense, we use those fighting foundations while accounting for weapons, surroundings, other people, and the need to disengage.

Understand the difference ↗
02

SKILL IS PERISHABLE.

One class can introduce a skill. Consistent practice helps you keep it. Timing, movement, judgment, and conditioning can fade without training, even when you've learned them before.

Make skill practice part of your routine so you are better prepared for the situations you hope never happen.
